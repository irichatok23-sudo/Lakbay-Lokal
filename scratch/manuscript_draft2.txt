Guillem School of Technology and Computer Studies Biliran Province State University Caibiran, Biliran lemeulguillem01@gmail.com Introduction T ourism has become one of the key factors of economic growth, community development, and cultural preservation. It creates employment opportunities, generates income for local communities, and contributes to regional development. Moreover, tourism can be a strategic tool for improving livelihoods in rural and local destinations, promoting cultural and environmental sustainability [1]. Therefore, many countries utilized Community-Based Tourism (or CBT) as a strategy that put local communities at the center of tourism planning, management and development processes. CBT emphasizes community participation, local ownership, and equitable distribution of tourism benefits and opportunities. According to Jackson [1], CBT plays a key role in alleviating poverty and economic growth, as it can create employment opportunities, support local entrepreneurship, and community resilience. At the same time, Herrera et al. [2] indicated that CBT can provide communities with an opportunity to improve local economy while ensuring environmental conservation and cultural preservation. Through CBT, local residents are key stakeholders in tourism development, rather than being passive beneficiaries of economic benefits. Advancements in digital technologies have transformed tourism destinations through tourism information systems, digital platforms, mobile applications, and online promotion activities, which have improved information accessibility for tourists and tourism management efficiency [3], [4]. The digital transformation has supported communities in promoting local tourism destinations, providing high-quality tourism information, and engaging in innovative ways with tourists [5]. However, many local tourism destinations still face challenges in tourism promotion, information accessibility for tourists, and tourism management. Several studies reported that local tourism communities face difficulties while promoting their destinations, providing information for tourists, and managing tourism activities [3], [6]. Falcon and Dugaria [4] argued that rural tourism destinations face significant difficulties while providing accessible and reliable tourism information, which limits their ability to market their destinations and attract tourists. At the same time, Ruangnarab et al. [7] indicated that the lack of integrated tourism information systems impedes effective collaboration among stakeholders and tourism management. Furthermore, successful tourism development requires effective collaboration, information exchange, and partnerships among local communities, tourism operators, government agencies, and stakeholders. Sihotang et al. [8] emphasized that tourism ecosystems that promote stakeholder collaboration and information exchange can facilitate community-based sustainable tourism development. In addition, studies on smart tourism villages and community-based digital tourism indicated that digital literacy, digital inclusiveness, and stakeholder participation present significant barriers to effective community-based digital tourism development [5], [9]. Finally, recent studies indicated that new technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI) and recommendation systems could be utilized to improve tourism services and information provision for tourists [10]. Moreover, studies on tourism information systems and digital community-based tourism suggest that such solutions could facilitate integrated and sustainable community-based tourism development [10], [11]. Given the importance of integrated tourism information systems and current gaps in local tourism promotion, this study was conducted to design a solution for promoting local community-based tourism destinations, supporting local tourism stakeholders, and facilitating sustainable community-based tourism development in the local destination. Objectives of the study General Objective This study aims to design and develop Lakbay Local, a Community-Based Tourism Information System, which will promote local tourist destinations and contribute to community-based tourism and the sustainable development of local tourism through community participation and digital tourism technologies. Specific objectives This study specifically aims to: Ascertain the current problems encountered in the provision of tourism information and the promotion of local tourist destinations in the selected community. Determine the information needs and requirements of tourists, local communities, tourism operators, and local government units for the proposed tourism information system. Design and develop Lakbay Local, a Community-Based Tourism Information System, which will provide information on: Tourist destinations; Local cultural and tourism-related activities; Community tourism services; and Tourism promotion; Establish a centralized platform for the management of tourism information, which will facilitate the dissemination of accurate and updated tourism-related information. Promote and increase community-based tourism promotion and management capabilities using the developed digital tourism information system. Evaluate the developed system according to its functional, usability, efficiency, and user satisfaction criteria using established software quality assurance standards. Ascertain the potential contribution of the developed Community-Based Tourism Information System towards sustainable tourism development by increasing visibility, stakeholder collaboration, and information accessibility. review of literated literature Community-Based Tourism and Sustainable Development Community-Based Tourism (CBT) is an emerging approach to tourism development that promotes community-based tourism planning and management practices. Jackson [1] argued that CBT contributes to Sustainable Development Goal 1 (No Poverty) and Sustainable Development Goal 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth) by creating employment opportunities and strengthening local economies. The study further revealed that community ownership and equitable benefit distribution are fundamental aspects of sustainable tourism development. Herrera et al. [2] agreed that CBT promotes economic development while conserving cultural heritage and environmental resources. Their findings revealed that communities can benefit from tourism through local business opportunities, employment creation, and effective tourism management practices. However, the study further revealed that lack of tourism awareness, inadequate tourism management, and poor promotion strategies could hinder sustainable tourism development. Susada [12] studied community-based tourism in Cebu, Philippines, and revealed that stakeholder participation is an integral component of tourism management. The study emphasized the need for community participation, government support, and effective marketing strategies in promoting tourism development and community engagement. Community Participation and Empowerment Community participation is a critical success factor in tourism development. Lapuz [13] argued that digital transformation can facilitate community empowerment by promoting community participation in tourism activities and decision-making processes. The study advocated for the need to use technology to enable communities to play an active role in tourism development rather than adopting automation approaches to tourism management. Ponwiritthon and Kamjai [14] revealed the role of participatory tourism information system in promoting tourism development. The study recommended the use of participatory approaches to collect tourism information and engage local communities in tourism development activities. Findings from the study further revealed that community participation in tourism systems promotes community ownership of tourism activities and sustainable tourism development. Tourism Information Systems Tourism information systems have become an integral part of modern tourism promotion, management, and development practices. Treemongkol and Punyayaw [6] developed a community tourism information system that facilitated effective tourism communication, information exchange, and tourism promotion approaches in the study area. Their findings revealed that digital information systems facilitated increased accessibility of tourism information and promoted community-based tourism activities. Falcon and Dugaria [4] developed an e-tourism portal for rural municipalities in the Philippines and discovered that tourism websites could effectively promote and market destinations while providing quality information to internet users. Similarly, Tuhuteru et al. [3] developed a web-based tourism management information system that contained destination, accommodation, and transportation information, as well as systems for online booking and monitoring of tourism activities. Findings from the study revealed that information systems promoted effective destination management, tourism promotion, and tourism service delivery. Centralized Tourism Management Tourism stakeholders increasingly adopt centralized tourism information management systems to promote standardization of tourism activities and enhance coordination of tourism-related activities. Ruangnarab et al. [7] developed a centralized tourism information system that connected tourists and local leadership in promoting community-based tourism in the study area. Findings from the study revealed that centralized information systems enhanced effective information management and stakeholder coordination in tourism development. Similarly, Tuhuteru et al. [3] revealed that centralized tourism management information systems could help tourism administrators manage tourism activities, monitor tourist arrivals, and coordinate other tourism-related activities in the study area. Smart Tourism and Digital Transformation Smart tourism is gaining popularity worldwide as a transformative force in community-based tourism development. Geohansa et al. [5] discovered that smart tourism practices promote effective destination branding and community participation in tourism development. However, the study further revealed that lack of robust digital infrastructure, inadequate digital literacy, and governance challenges could impede the effective adoption of smart tourism across different communities and destinations. The Digital Community-Based Tourism study in Ciamis Regency [9] discovered that digital technologies promote community-based tourism through increased community visibility and economic development. However, the study recommended further research to reveal the role of digital inclusion and accessibility in enhancing community-based tourism. Stakeholder Collaboration and Tourism Governance Effective collaboration and governance mechanisms are crucial for achieving sustainable tourism development. Sihotang et al. [8] recommended effective e-collaboration platforms among communities, government agencies, tourism operators, and private stakeholders to enhance sustainable tourism development in the study area. Geohansa et al. [5] further revealed the importance of effective governance frameworks in smart tourism development, emphasizing the need for effective collaboration and coordination mechanisms for successful smart tourism governance among stakeholders. Advanced Tourism Technologies Modern technologies such as artificial intelligence are gradually being adopted in tourism management and destination development. Kulkarineetham and Supanich [15] revealed that the application of personalized recommendation systems enhanced tourist recommendations for different tourism activities and attractions, thereby promoting sustainable tourism development in the study area. Durrah et al. [10] recommended the adoption of advanced technologies such as AI technologies, IoT, and QR coding in tourism development activities. The study revealed that such technologies could enhance effective information delivery, improve tourism operational performance, and facilitate effective tourism management practices in different tourism destinations. Potjanajaruwit et al. [11] proposed an AI tourism application that integrated community information, tourism events, travel routes, accommodation, transportation, food, and tourism activities to enhance service delivery and promote effective community-based tourism in the study area. method This section presents the research design and procedures used in the development of Lakbay Lokal: A Community-Based Tourism Information System. Specifically, it discusses the research design, system development, system architecture, research locale, respondents of the study, research instruments, data gathering procedure and data analysis procedure used in the study. The aforementioned research procedures will be used to determine the information and management needs of local tourism communities which will be used as a basis in the development of the proposed system. Research Design The researchers propose to use a developmental research design for the development of Lakbay Lokal: A Community-Based Tourism Information System. The reason for choosing this approach is that developmental research involves the systematic design, development, testing, and evaluation of a technological system to solve a specific problem. The study will address the identified problem regarding information management in local communities. Information on local tourism activities and services is gathered informally and kept in various formats, making it difficult for communities, local government units, and workers to monitor tourism participation, transactions, and activities. Local workers may lack transparency on their tourism-related earnings, and barangays and LGUs may find it challenging to have organized information for monitoring and planning. To address these concerns, the researchers propose to develop Lakbay Lokal: A Community-Based Tourism Information System. This proposed system will provide tourism information to tourists while providing participating local communities with tools for registration, verification, and monitoring of tourism activities. The community-based tourism information system will also provide management and reporting functions for the Barangay, LGU, and Tourism Office. The study will gather information from tourists and local tourism workers or community participants, barangay representatives, LGU representatives, and Tourism Office representatives. The results will be used to determine the requirements of the proposed system and will form the basis of its design and development. The developed system will go through a series of functional testing, user evaluation, and user acceptance testing. The results will be used to determine whether the selected system meets the specified information-management and community tourism needs. A.1 System Development The researchers propose to use the Agile development methodology in the development of Lakbay Lokal. The reason for choosing this approach is that it allows the system to be developed iteratively. This means that requirements can be reviewed, features can be tested, and improvements can be made based on feedback. The iterative nature of Agile is more appropriate for Lakbay Lokal because the system will have multiple users, namely tourists, local tourism workers, barangays, LGUs, and the Tourism Office. Each user has different information and functional requirements. The incremental approach will allow the researchers to identify and incorporate these requirements into the system development process. Figure 1. (Agile Development Cycle) The development process will involve the following phases: Requirements Gathering The researchers will gather information from the identified stakeholders to determine their needs, problems, and expectations in regards to tourism information management. Tourists will provide information regarding the tourism information they need when exploring local destinations and activities. Local tourism workers will provide information regarding their tourism activities, transactions, and monitoring needs. Barangay, LGU, and Tourism Office representatives will provide information regarding registration, verification, monitoring, reporting, and tourism management requirements. Planning and System Design The information gathered during the requirements-gathering phase will be analyzed and converted into system requirements. The researchers will determine the system's user roles, information structure, database requirements, interfaces, and major functions. The proposed system will have separate functions suitable for tourists, local tourism workers/community participants, barangays, LGUs, the Tourism Office, and system administrators. Development and Implementation The researchers will develop Lakbay Lokal based on the identified requirements. The system will be incrementally implemented with the required features, including tourism information, community participant registration and verification, tourism activity management, booking and transaction monitoring, and report generation. Testing Each developed feature will be tested to determine whether it fulfills its intended purpose. The researchers will identify errors, incorrect outputs, navigation problems, and other technical issues and address them during the subsequent development cycle. User Evaluation and Feedback Selected respondents representing the intended users and stakeholders will interact with the developed system. They will evaluate the system based on the criteria established by the researchers. Feedback will be gathered to determine areas that require improvement. Improvement The researchers will analyze the results of the testing and user-evaluation and implement necessary improvements. Another iteration of the system development process will be conducted if necessary until the identified requirements have been sufficiently addressed. A.2 System Architecture Lakbay Lokal will employ a three-layer system architecture that will consist of the User/Client Interface Layer, Application/System Layer, and Database Layer. The architecture will organize the system's user interfaces, processing functions, and stored information. Figure 2. (System Architecture Diagram) The User/Client Interface Layer will provide the interfaces through which users and tourism stakeholders will interact with Lakbay Lokal. Tourists will be able to access information about community-based tourism activities and local offerings. Local tourism workers will be able to manage relevant information and monitor their bookings and transactions. The Barangay, LGU, and Tourism Office will have access to functions appropriate to their respective monitoring and management responsibilities. The Application/System Layer will process the requests and activities performed by users. This layer will contain the main system functions, including authentication, participant registration, verification, tourism activity management, booking and transaction monitoring, report generation, and statistics. The proposed management flow will follow the system’s organizational structure; The Tourism Office will have access to consolidated tourism information and reports for broader tourism monitoring and planning. The LGU will manage and monitor tourism-related information within its jurisdiction. The Barangay will handle community-level registration, verification, and monitoring of participating local tourism workers. Local tourism workers or community participants will provide tourism activities and services that can be presented through the system. Tourists will access the available tourism information and community-based experiences. The Database Layer will store the information required by the system, including user accounts, community participants, tourism activities, booking records, transaction records, verification information, and reports. For example, when a local tourism worker registers in the system, the submitted information will be processed by the application layer and stored in the database. The appropriate barangay-level user can review the participant information for verification. Verified information can become available within the tourism information system. When tourists access tourism activities and make bookings, the resulting records can be stored and used by the appropriate stakeholders for monitoring and reporting. The architecture therefore supports the centralized management of tourism information while maintaining the roles of the Tourism Office, LGU, Barangay, local tourism workers, and tourists. Research Locale The study will be conducted in Biliran Province located in the Philippines. Biliran will serve as the research locale because the proposed system is intended to support community-based tourism information management in the area. The study will cover tourism communities, local tourism workers, barangays, LGUs, and tourism offices in the province. These stakeholders are critical to the research because they provide, manage, promote, monitor, and utilize tourism-related information. Biliran can be considered an appropriate locale for developing Lakbay Lokal because the proposed system aims to highlight the community-based tourism activities and events in the area to promote them better. Additionally, it will allow the researchers to gather data from local stakeholders who will use the system as its primary target audience. The study will also consider the current practices in collecting, managing, and sharing tourism information among local stakeholders. The information gathered from the research locale will help satisfy the system’s requirements by identifying those that are critical and necessary to address during development. Respondents of the Study The study's respondents will consist of individuals from the primary stakeholder groups of the proposed Lakbay Lokal system. These include tourists /tourism information seekers, local tourism workers/community participants, Barangay representatives, LGU representatives, and Tourism Office representatives. Tourists/Tourism Information Seekers Tourists and information seekers will be asked to share their experiences and needs regarding destination, activities, local services, and community-based experiences. They will be asked for tourism information they usually seek, factors in selecting tourism activities, difficulties in obtaining local tourism information; preferred tourism information, preferred system features. Local tourism workers/Community participants Local tourism workers and community participants will be asked to share their tourism activities and current process of managing bookings, transactions, and tourism-related information. They will provide information on types of tourism activities or services offered, current booking methods, difficulties in monitoring transactions, information needed for managing tourism activities, participant registration and verification; and evaluation of the system. Barangay representatives Barangay representatives will be asked to share information on community-level tourism management. They will be asked about: registration of local tourism workers; verification of participants; monitoring of community tourism activities; information needed from local participants; monitoring of bookings and transactions; and reports required at the barangay level. Barangay representatives Barangay representatives will be asked to share information on community-level tourism management. They will be asked about: registration of local tourism workers; verification of participants; monitoring of community tourism activities; information needed from local participants; monitoring of bookings and transactions; and reports required at the barangay level. Barangay representatives Barangay representatives will be asked to share information on community-level tourism management. They will be asked about: registration of local tourism workers; verification of participants; monitoring of community tourism activities; information needed from local participants; monitoring of bookings and transactions; and reports required at the barangay level. Samplimg Method The researchers will use purposive sampling in selecting respondents. This method will be used because the study requires participants who have direct experience or involvement in tourism information, community-based tourism, tourism management, or the use of tourism-related services. The number of respondents will be determined according to the scope of the study and the availability of qualified participants. The researchers will establish the final number of respondents for each stakeholder group before data collection. Research Instrument The researchers will employ several research instruments to gather the information needed for the development and evaluation of Lakbay Lokal . A structured questionnaire will be administered to tourists and tourism information seekers to identify their information needs, preferences, difficulties, and expectations. Questionnaires or interview guides will also be used with local tourism workers and community participants to determine their current practices in managing tourism activities, bookings, transactions, and participant information. Interview guides will be conducted with Barangay, LGU, and Tourism Office representatives to identify their registration, verification, monitoring, reporting, and tourism planning requirements. An observation and document-review checklist will be used to examine existing tourism information-management processes. After development, a system evaluation questionnaire and user acceptance and task-testing checklist will be used to assess the system's functionality, usability, efficiency, reliability, information quality, user satisfaction, and task completion. Data Analysis Procedure The data gathered from the respondents will be analyzed using appropriate descriptive statistical and qualitative techniques based on the objectives of the study. Frequency and Percentage Frequency and percentage will be used to summarize categorical data such as respondent profiles, tourism information needs, preferred activities, and sources of information. The percentage will be calculated as: Percentage = (Frequency / Total Number of Respondents) × 100 Weighted Mean Weighted mean will be used to determine the level of importance of tourism information, system requirements, and the respondents’ evaluation of Lakbay Lokal based on the selected evaluation criteria. Thematic Analysis Information gathered from interviews and observations will be grouped into common themes to identify problems, needs, practices, and suggestions. These findings will help determine the system requirements of tourists, local tourism workers, Barangays, LGUs, and the Tourism Office. System Functional Analysis The researchers will assess the system’s ability to manage bookings, transactions, registration, verification, reports, and statistics. The results will determine whether the system can properly record, retrieve, and display required information for authorized users. Task Completion Analysis Task testing results will be analyzed based on successfully completed tasks, unsuccessful tasks, errors, difficulties, and functions needing improvement. The task completion rate will be calculated as: Task Completion Rate = (Successfully Completed Tasks / Total Assigned Tasks) × 100 System Evaluation The system evaluation questionnaire will be summarized using the weighted mean and interpreted according to the evaluation scale established by the researchers. The results from questionnaires, interviews, observations, functional testing, and task testing will be used to assess whether Lakbay Lokal meets the identified tourism-information and community-management needs.
