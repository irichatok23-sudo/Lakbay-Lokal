Guillem School of Technology and Computer Studies Biliran Province State University Caibiran, Biliran lemuelguillem01@gmail.com Introduction T ourism support the local economies by creating a job, providing income and community development. It helps also to preserve the cultural practices and the natural resources, especially in rural destination s [1]. A Community-Based Tourism (CBT) follows an approach involving the local residents in tourism planning, management, and activities. Encourages communities to participate in tourism development and gain benefits from the opportunities it provides. A CBT enforce local participation, ownership, and fair sharing in tourism benefits. Jackson [1] discussed on how the CBT can support employment, local businesses, and the involvement of the community. Herrera et al. [2] also examined how tourism strengthen the local economies while helping to protect the cultural and environmental resources. These Studies highlight the importance of giving a residents an active role in tourism development. The digital technology has changed on how destinations share an information and promote tourism. Websites and information system can make destination easier to access and organize tourism-related activities [3], [4]. Digital platforms can give communitie s more ways to present their attractions, activities, and services they can offer to potential visitors [5]. However, local destinations may still facing a difficulties involving keeping tourism information accessible, updated, and, organized. Falcon and Dugaria [4] examined that information-access challenges in countryside municipalities, while, Ruangnarab et al. [6] discussed the information management in community-based tourism. Tourism development may requires an operation among the residents, tourism, operators, and government offices. Research on community -based and a smart tourism emphasized the stakeholder collaboration, digital inclusion, and community participation [5], [7], [8]. Othe studies have explored the artificial intelligence and a recommendation system as a ways to improve tourism information and services [9], [10]. The developments show the potential of technology, but, digital system must address the needs and a capabilities of their intended users. At Biliran Province, tourism information and the records may be collected and maintained through different practices. This situation may make it difficult for the local workers as well the government offices to organize information and monitor tourism activities, booking, and a transactions. An organized approach could help stakeholders shares tourism information and support the local monitoring and planning. To address these concerns, the researchers propose Lakbay -Lokal: A Community-Based Tourism Information System, The system provide tourism information to visitors and support local communities through stakeholder registration, verification , service management, booking and monitoring, and reporting. It designed to connect tourist, local tourism stakeholders, Barangay officials, local government units (LGUs), and the Tourism Office through functions to their respective responsibilities. O bjectives of the Study The general objective of this study is to design and develop Lakbay -Lokal, a Community-Based Tourism Information System that promotes local destinations and supports community-based tourism through community participation and digital technology Specfically, the study aims to: Identify the problems encountered in providing tourism information and promoting local destinations in Biliran Province. Determine the information and management needs of tourist, local tourism stakeholders, Barangays, LGUs, and the Tourism Office. Design and develop a system that provides a destination information, community tourism services, activities, and bookings functions. Develop role-based functions for stakeholders registrations and verification, tourism activity management, booking monitoring and report generatio n. Review Related The reviewed studies present a different perspectives about community-based tourism particularly to relations of community participation, sustainable development, tourism information management, and digital transformation . Jackson [1] and Herrera et al.[2] discuss a role of community-based tourism in supporting local livelihoods and responsible use of cultural and natural resources. Similarly, Susada [11] examines a involvement of stakeholders in community-based tourism, while Lapuz [12] and Ponwiritthon and Kamjai [13] emphasizing the community participation and empowerment in tourism development. Although these studies approach the subject from different perspectives, the view they share about local communities play an imprtant role in tourism planing and management. In term of technology and information management, the studies focuses on different ways of improving tourism services and access to informations . Treemongkol and Punayayaw [14] developed the community tourist information system, while Falcon and Dugaria [4] focuses on the use of a tourism website for improving information accessibility in rural municipalities. Tuhuteru et al. [3] examined a web-based village tourism management system, that addresses the organization of tourism information and management activities. Ruangnarab et al. [6] explored also the centralized information management for community-based tourism, These studies demonstrate on how information system can assist in organizing tourism-related information, although their settings and areas of focus differ. Research on digital transformation provides further insight about the conditions that affect the use of technology in community-based tourism. Geohansa et al.[5] found out that digital activities in the tourism villages they studied were mainly focuses on social media promotion, with a limited use of integrated reservation and tourism data management systems. They identified also a unstable internet infrastracture and limited digital literacy as challenges. Similarly, Nursetiawan et al . [8] examined digital inclusivity in community-based tourism in Ciamis Regency, focused on the conditions that affect the community’s access to and use of digital technology. These studies show that adopting a digital techbnology involves more than providing and online platform, the read iness and a participation of the community is also needed to be considered. Other studies give attention to a stakeholder coordination and emerging technologies. Sihotang et al. [7] talks about the collaboration and information exchange in the groups involved in community-based tourism. Durrah et al. [9] review the opportunities and challenges of digital technologies in sustainable community-based tourism. Potjanajaruwit et al. [10] examine a application of artificial intelligence in tourism, while Kulkarineetham and Supanich [15] focuses on personalizing tourist recommendations and community-based tourism management. These works differ on technological approaches, but they demonstrate the growing use of digital tools to support tourism services, information sharing, and the destination ma n agement. Overall, the reviewed studies show that community-based tourism depends on both active community involvement and effective tourism management. Some emphasize social and economic role of a local participation , while others focus on information systems, digital access, stakeholder coordination, and the technology-based services. These studies provides the basis for the development of Lakbay Lokal. The proposed system applies a relevant ideas from the review s studies by providing tourism services and coordination of a stakeholders involved in tourism activities in Biliran Province. Methodolog Research Design This study will use a developmental research design because it involves designing, developing, testing, and evaluating a system. The researchers will identify the needs of intented users and use the gathered information as the basis for developing Lakbay Lokal. System Development Methodology Agile methodology will be used during the development process that allows the researchers to check the requirements, test the features of the system and improve based on feedbacks. The following activities will be performed during the development: Requirements Gathering Information will be collected from tourist, local tourism workers, barangays, LGUs and Tourism Office to determine the needs of users. Planning Design The information will be used to determine the roles of users, features of the system, interface, and requirement of the database. Testing The features were tested to identify any errors, wrong outputs and navigation issues. User Evaluation The system will be used by the selected respondents and will be evaluated based on the criteria identified. Improvement Results from testing and evaluation will be used to make the necessary changes. Fig. 1 Agile Development Cycle. System Architecture Lakbay Lokal will be usin g a three-layer architecture composed of the User/Client Interface Layer, Application/System Layer, and Database Layer. The User/Client Interface Layer will provide the screens and functions that will serve the tourist, local tourism stakeholders, Barangays, LGUs and Tourism Office. Each users will be provided with their own specific functions depending upon u pon their roles The Application/System Layer will handle the requests made by the users. This includes authentication, registering, verifying, managing tourism activities, booking, transactions and generating reports. The Barangay will manage and verify registrations at the community level. The LGU and Tourism Office will have access to information. The Database Layer will store users, stakeholder, destinations, tourism activities, booking, transactions, verification and reports. For example, local tourism stakeholders will register and this will be stored and viewed by the relevant Barangay user. After verification, the approved stakeholder profile and services can be made available to tourists. Fig, 2: Three-Tiered System Architecture. Research Locale The study will be conducted in Biliran Province, Philippines. The locale is selected because the system that we are proposing will help in promoting community-based tourism as well as helping organize and share tourism information within the local community and government offices. Respondents The study will involve 100 respondents coming from the following stakeholders: Tourist of tourism Information: Provide information on what kind of tourism information they look for, what activities they are interested in and their difficulties in finding tourism infor m ation in the locality Barangay Officials: Provide information on what their needs are in registering, verifying, monitoring and creating reports among the stakeholders. L GU Officials: Provide information on the records they have in relation to tourism, monitoring, statistics , planning and coordination requirements Tourism Office Officials: Provide information on what they need in promoting tourism in the province, compiling of monitoring reports and planning. Purposive sampling will be conducted to determine respondents who have experience or directly involved in tourism in tourism information, community-based tourism management and tourism related services. Research Instruments The researchers will use questionnaires, interview guides, and an observation and document review checklist. T he structured questionnaire will be used to gather information about tourist information needs, preferences and problems. The tourism employees in the locality will be asked questions about their services, bookings, and monitoring of transactions. The interview guides will be used to gather information about the information needs of the Barangay, LGU and Tourism Office representatives regarding registration, validation, monitoring, reporting, and planning. A System Evaluation Questionnaire will be used to analyze how functional, user-friendly, efficient and satisfying the system is. A Likert Scale will be used for the System Evaluation Questionnaire, with the options being: 5 – Strongly Agree, 4 – Agree, 3 – Neutral, 2 – Disagree, 1 – Strongly Disagree. Data Gathering The researchers will communicate with the concerned parties and ask them to participate in the study. Questionnaires and interviews will be administered to the identified respondents. Meanwhile, observation and documentation will be used to gather information on how the tourism information is currently gathered, organi z ed and managed. This information will provide the basis of the system requirements. When Lakbay Lokal has been developed, the chosen respondents will be asked to use the system. Results will be gathered based on how they respond to the system, what is observed and what results are obtained when carrying out the tasks based on their roles. Data Analysis The gathered data will be analyzed using descriptive statistical and qualitative method based on the objectives of the study. Frequency and Percentage Frequency and percentage will be used to summarize the categorical data collected on respondents’ profile, information needs of tourism, prefe r red activity and information sources. Percentage can be calculated using the following formula: Percentage = (Frequency / Total Number of respondents) x 100 Weighted Mean Weighted mean will be used to summarize the rating given by respondents on the information needs of tourism, system requirements and system evaluation using the five point Likert scale. Thematic Analysis Responses from interviews and observations will be classified into different themes. This will help to identify any common issues, needs, current procedures and suggestions. Functionality Analysis The researchers will test if the system functions correctly according to its key functions. This includes registering, verifying, managing bookings, tracking transactions and generating reports. Task Analysis Tasks will be tested by measuring how many tasks were successfully completed as well as any errors and issues encountered. The task completion rate is calculated using the following formula: Task Completion Rate = (Tasks successfully completed / Total Number of tasks assigned) x 100 System Evaluation The weighted mean of the evaluations will be taken along with feedback from interviews, observations, functionality testing and task analysis. This will help to determine if the system fulfils the users’ needs identified and what areas need improving. References L. A.
